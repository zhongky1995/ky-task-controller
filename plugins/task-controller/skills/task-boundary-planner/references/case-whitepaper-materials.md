# Case / Whitepaper Material Reference

Use this reference when the task is to plan a case study, whitepaper source package, public-facing case supply draft, market-facing proof pack, or evidence-heavy case material curation.

This reference is not for final writing. It defines what must be true before writing starts.

## Core Distinction

First classify the deliverable:

- **Whitepaper source contribution**: curated case facts, method logic, result evidence, and usable visuals for an editor to rewrite.
- **Public case draft**: near-publishable case narrative for external readers.
- **Internal case memo**: internal learning, evidence notes, risks, and material map.
- **Client proposal case proof**: case selected to prove a point for a specific client decision.
- **Archive/material pack**: raw files and source materials for future retrieval.

Do not mix these. A whitepaper source contribution is not an archive, internal report, or sales proposal.

## Reader Stack

State all three layers:

- **Requester**: the team asking for material.
- **Direct user**: editor, strategy lead, market team, client team, or internal reviewer who will adapt it.
- **Final reader**: external industry reader, client decision-maker, internal management, or project team.

Background shared with the assistant is not automatically wording for the final artifact. Internal addressees such as "总部", "老板", or "客户组" may be context only.

## Evidence Status

Classify every important source and claim before drafting:

```text
案例素材分层
- 可写成案例事实:
- 可写成结果数据:
- 可抽象成方法论:
- 只能后台参考:
- 不得进入正文:
- 需要发布前确认:
```

Rules:

- Delivered work, signed-off project outputs, and verified result data may support case facts.
- Draft proposals, test plans, sales decks, incomplete forms, and unclosed opportunities cannot prove delivered work.
- A source can inspire a method abstraction without being described as "what happened in the case."
- Raw file existence is not material quality. Files must prove a claim, explain a method, or support a visual need.
- Screenshots and attachments are not proof unless their role in the story is clear.

## Case Object Model

Before writing, define:

- Case subject: brand, category, business problem, project status, and disclosure boundary.
- Problem: the specific business issue the case answers.
- Delivered actions: what actually happened.
- Method abstraction: what reusable method can be inferred from the delivered actions.
- Result evidence: numbers, time range, comparison baseline, and source reliability.
- Usable materials: charts, screenshots, diagrams, quotes, tables, and examples worth showing.
- Forbidden materials: internal notes, raw attachments, unclosed proposal details, operational clutter, confidential terms, and unverified claims.

## Claim Support Gate

Run this for central claims only:

```text
案例结论校准
- 结论:
- 证据等级: 已交付事实 / 结果数据 / 方法论抽象 / 假设 / 不支持
- 证据来源:
- 不能写成:
- 安全写法:
- 可配图/表格:
```

If the result is `方法论抽象`, do not phrase it as a direct project fact.
If the result is `假设` or `不支持`, keep it out of the final artifact unless it appears as a next-step question.

## Material Curation Standard

Use fewer, stronger materials.

Include only materials that satisfy at least one:

- Proves a central result.
- Explains the method better than text.
- Shows a concrete delivered action.
- Helps the editor build a chart or case page.

Exclude:

- Raw archive dumps.
- Attachments included only to appear "complete."
- Drafts with internal wording or unresolved assumptions.
- Repeated screenshots that prove the same point.
- Process files, QA leftovers, path indexes, file lists, or contact sheets unless the deliverable is an archive.

## Section Unit Contract

For whitepaper source contribution, plan these units:

| Unit | Task | Required evidence | Must not do | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Case summary | Explain the case in 50-150 words | Subject, problem, action, result | Mention internal requester or source folders | Editor can lift it directly |
| Business problem | Name the concrete market/customer issue | Category context and case facts | Open with generic industry slogans | Reader understands why the case matters |
| Delivered actions | Show what was actually done | Signed-off output or reliable project record | Mix in unclosed proposal tactics | Actions are concrete and attributable |
| Method abstraction | Turn actions into reusable logic | Delivered actions as base | Claim unsupported "framework" authority | Method is useful but not overclaimed |
| Result evidence | Present outcome and caveats | Metric, baseline, period, source | Turn weak data into guaranteed results | Numbers are traceable and safely worded |
| Visual/material list | Select usable visuals | Each visual maps to a claim | Dump all images/files | 2-4 strong visuals are enough |
| Publish checks | Flag disclosure risks | Names, data, images, claims | Hide risky assumptions | Reviewer knows what to confirm |

## Visual Rules

Recommended visual set:

- 1 result chart.
- 1 method/action diagram.
- 1 table that summarizes actions or stages.
- 0-2 screenshots only if they are clean and directly support the case.

Do not use visuals that contain internal file names, draft labels, "test", "unclosed", "for internal", QA notes, or irrelevant UI clutter.

## Acceptance Checks

Before production, the contract should answer:

- What is the artifact: source contribution, public case draft, internal memo, proposal proof, or archive?
- Who is the direct user and who is the final reader?
- Which claims are delivered facts, result data, method abstraction, or unsupported?
- Which source materials are excluded even if they exist?
- Which visuals are selected and what claim does each support?
- What must be confirmed before external publication?

If these cannot be answered, stop at evidence acquisition or source ledger. Do not draft the final case.
